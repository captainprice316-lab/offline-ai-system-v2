{\Large\bfseries Correction Memo --- VANI ASR Fine-Tuning Results}\\[2pt]
{\small 12 July 2026, addendum 13 July \quad$\cdot$\quad supersedes the fine-tuning figures reported prior to 10 July 2026}
\end{center}

\vspace{2pt}
\textbf{Summary.} During the construction of a WER-under-degradation evaluation harness, I found two independent
defects in the scoring pipeline behind the previously reported fine-tuning results. Both are fixed, all
evaluations were re-run ($n{=}100$ held-out FLEURS test samples per language), and the corrected numbers change
the study's conclusion: per-language Whisper fine-tuning is justified only for Pashto and Kashmiri; for the
other five languages zero-shot SeamlessM4T is superior and is now the deployed backend.

\textbf{Defect 1 --- wrong baseline model.} The comparison script loaded \texttt{whisper-large-v3-turbo} while
labelling it \texttt{large-v3}. Every published baseline was therefore inflated, overstating fine-tuning gains
across all languages (worst: Urdu's gain was $-4.6$~pp, truly $-1.4$~pp; Punjabi's $-50$~pp, truly $-20$~pp).

\textbf{Defect 2 --- whitespace WER on Mandarin.} FLEURS Mandarin references are character-spaced; WER tokenises
on whitespace; un-fine-tuned models emit unspaced Han. A near-perfect transcript therefore scored ${\sim}100\%$
WER. The headline ``Mandarin $100.03\% \rightarrow 16.03\%$ ($-84$~pp)'' was entirely this artefact. Re-scored
with character segmentation, fine-tuning \emph{regressed} Mandarin: baseline $10.99\%$, fine-tuned $14.22\%$.
The earlier explanation that the baseline ``translates Chinese by default'' was wrong and is withdrawn.

\textbf{The corrected result is a stronger paper, not a weaker one.} The pattern is now interpretable:
fine-tuning helps in proportion to how poorly the base model covers the language (Pashto $-51$~pp, Kashmiri
$-23$~pp with a custom \texttt{<|ks|>} token) and can hurt when the base is already strong (Mandarin $+3.2$~pp
regression from a small single-domain training set). A 30-clip $\times$ 5-condition robustness sweep (clean,
300--3400~Hz bandpass, 10/0~dB SNR noise, MP3 codec) shows SeamlessM4T's advantage holds and \emph{widens} under
degradation (Hindi $+5.0 \rightarrow +19.5$~pp at 0~dB), so the backend-selection conclusion is safe for
operational radio --- a more defensible contribution than the original per-language fine-tuning claim.

\textbf{Addendum (13 July) --- Defect 3, fine-tuning label encoding.} A third defect was found in the
SeamlessM4T fine-tuning script (not the scoring pipeline). Training labels were tokenised as plain text,
which SeamlessM4T's tokenizer encodes in \emph{source} mode --- prefix \texttt{\_\_eng\_\_} --- regardless
of the requested target language; target-mode encoding (prefix \texttt{[eos, \_\_lang\_\_]}, what
\texttt{generate()} forces at inference) requires the \texttt{text\_target=} argument. All six SeamlessM4T
LoRA adapters were therefore trained against mismatched prefixes. The effect was invisible in ASR (the model
learned ``emit source-language text after any prefix'', which happens to be correct for transcription) but
destroyed translation: the near-zero S2TT chrF (0.06--2.12) previously reported as ``LoRA adaptation
overrides multi-task language conditioning'' was entirely this bug, and that explanation is withdrawn.
A controlled retrain of the Pashto adapter with corrected labels changed nothing else: ASR WER 41.30 vs.\
41.22 (identical), chrF $2.12 \rightarrow 37.60$ (near the zero-shot 40.15). The pattern matches Defects
1--2: a silent methodological error producing plausible headline numbers and a wrong causal story ---
reinforcing the memo's central lesson rather than altering any deployment decision.

\textbf{Audit trail.} The correction is fully reproducible: pre-fix results are archived (the
\texttt{PRE\_FIX} JSONs in \texttt{docs/}), all re-runs persist raw per-utterance hypotheses to JSONL so
re-scoring never requires re-inference, one CJK-aware normaliser is used everywhere, and two automated proofread
scripts (298 checks total) verify every figure in the regenerated report, presentation, and paper sources
against the source data. The corrected routing (\texttt{seamless\_langs: pa, ne, hi, ur, zh}) is deployed and
verified end-to-end in the live pipeline.
